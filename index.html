Case study · PayGURU Technosoft · Mar 2026 to now

HRMS platform redesign

PayGURU makes HR, payroll and workforce software that works with biometric attendance devices. The previous version felt plain and its screens were scattered. As the only designer, I'm redesigning it module by module.

Role
UI/UX Design Intern (sole designer)
Timeline
Mar 2026 to present
Product
Web app and companion mobile app
Scope
35-40 screens, 7+ modules
Tools
Figma, ChatGPT, Gemini, Google Stitch

Screens are shown with the company's permission, with employee data blurred.

The modules I redesigned

  • Onboarding
  • Attendance management
  • Employee management
  • Contract-labour management
  • Canteen management
  • Travel management
  • Leave and tour management
  • Add-data forms

The problem

The old version felt too simple, and many things were scattered across separate screens. The company also wanted the product to look more polished and match its brand theme.

HR staff and employees at every client company use the web portal day to day. The mobile app is used only for attendance, and only by employees of client companies that asked for it.

What I changed

Merged screens

Combined screens that were split for no good reason, so related information and actions sit together.

Example: the masters had separate pages for Department, Sub-department and Section. I combined them into one Department page, with tabs to switch between the three.

Refreshed visuals

Updated the visual design to the theme the company wanted, so every module looks current and consistent.

I studied professional admin templates like Inspinia, Vuexy and Metronic for reference, then built a clean, consistent visual style across every module.

Built with developers

Reviewed designs with the development team, so screens could be built as designed.

Sitting with the developers to understand every feature showed me which screens shared functions and could be combined, and kept every design feasible.

Before and after

Click any screen to see it larger.

Mobile app: attendance check-in

Using AI to explore faster

I use ChatGPT, Gemini and Google Stitch to explore ideas quickly, then design the real screens in Figma.

For the mobile attendance check-in, I used ChatGPT and Gemini to brainstorm different ways to show location status and shift timings, and Google Stitch to generate early visual directions. I then built the final screens and prototype in Figma.

What I learned

AI lets me compare several layout directions in minutes instead of hours. But its output works best as rough inspiration: typography, consistency and fit with what users need still have to be designed by hand in Figma.

Hyperlocal marketplace app

A mobile app for Android and iOS that shows live offers from nearby shops and tracks the trip there, on foot, by cycle or by car. I built its design system from scratch and designed the core screens.

Role
UI/UX Designer (sole designer)
Timeline
Jun 2025 to Feb 2026
Tools

Platform

Confidential

The client moved the project to another team before development. The design system and user flows belong to the client, so this case study describes them in words and shows only the final screens.

The problem

There was no existing app, brand system or component library to build on. The client needed a complete product design that local shoppers could use without help, and that developers could build without guessing.

The app is for anyone nearby: residents, students, local business owners and tourists. The client's goal was to connect local shopkeepers with the people around them, so they discover live offers and walk, cycle or drive to the shop.

The core journey

  1. Onboarding
  2. OTP login
  3. Home
  4. Categories
  5. Offer and merchant details
  6. Cart
  7. Journey tracker

The design system

I built the foundations first (colour palette, typography and icons), then created components as the screens needed them, so every screen reused the same cards, forms and navigation patterns instead of one-off designs.

  • Colour palette
  • Typography
  • Icon set
  • Cards
  • Form fields
  • Navigation

Key decisions

Decision

Phone number and OTP login instead of passwords.

Why

The client wanted quick, secure access without the friction of passwords.

Decision

A journey tracker showing distance covered, cart items completed versus pending, travel mode (walking, cycling or driving), steps taken and approximate money saved.

Why

So users see their trip progress, how they're travelling and what they've saved, in real time.

Decision

Case study · LINQ Corporate Solutions · Dec 2024 to Apr 2025

Event email campaigns

For LINQ's events, I built and sent the campaign emails, and fixed bugs in the company's existing HTML email code so the emails displayed correctly.

Role
Web & Graphics Designer
Timeline
Dec 2024 to Apr 2025
Tools
VerticalResponse, HTML/CSS
Output
Email campaigns for company events

What I did

Built and sent

Used VerticalResponse's template builder to put together each event's email, with visuals from the in-house graphic design team, and sent it out.

Fixed the code

The company's existing HTML email code broke layouts in some inboxes, especially older desktop clients.

I replaced modern CSS flexbox wrappers with nested HTML tables and explicit cell padding, cleaned up broken table structures, fixed inline CSS and image alignment, so emails rendered properly in clients like Outlook and Gmail.

Why email HTML is tricky

  • About 600px wide, so it fits every inbox
  • Table-based layout, which email clients render reliably
  • Inline CSS, since many clients strip style blocks
  • Web-safe font fallbacks
  • Alt text on every image
  • Single-column stacking on phones
